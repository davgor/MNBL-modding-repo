# Combat callback and native continuation findings

2026-10-25. **Native body cleave is demonstrated in both swing directions; integrated shield mitigation and cleave is demonstrated for the left swing only. Right-swing shield continuation remains unresolved.** These are isolated custom-battle feasibility results, not production acceptance or campaign XP proof. Retained runtime logs distinguish unsuccessful approaches from the working route below.

## Controlled runtime findings and next experiment

- [Run 42180](../../evidence/combat-overhaul/runtime/combat/run-42180.jsonl): `Controller.None` plus explicit stock ready/release `SetActionChannel` calls produced accepted animations and native AttackRelease stages but no contacts. Animation playback alone does not establish attack collision ownership.
- [Run 39720](../../evidence/combat-overhaul/runtime/combat/run-39720.jsonl): `AgentControllerType.AI`, `AgentHumanAILogic`, and an owned `AgentComponent.OnAIInputSet` callback produced real attacks. `SetHasOnAiInputSetCallback(true)` lets the fixture replace native AI event flags, directional attack flags and movement vector after AI input generation. This avoids hardware input and the earlier player-controller contamination. A scheduled hold then release drives the native attack state. Native stage transitions, callback counts and collision progress identify the swing; trial names alone do not.
- [Run 21296](../../evidence/combat-overhaul/runtime/combat/run-21296.jsonl): directional AI defense produced actual shield contacts. Baseline shield contact caused zero body damage. Rewriting `AttackCollisionData` to body contact, reducing the native-created Blow to 35%, then returning ContinueChecking and momentum 1 caused mitigated body damage but still interrupted the swing. The attacker changed from ReleaseMelee to BlockedMelee. `OnAgentHitBlocked` fired after the hit callback even though its supplied collision now said StrikeAgent. The retained native collision job reads original collision-record fields after the managed callback and schedules a blocked action independently of the mutated struct. Late result/stun writes are insufficient for this path.
- [Run 46400](../../evidence/combat-overhaul/runtime/combat/run-46400.jsonl): the earlier native-to-managed `Mission.GetDefendCollisionResults` callback provides writable `crushedThrough` and attacker-stun outputs. The scoped intervention sets native crush-through true and attacker stun zero for fixture shield defenses. `MissionCombatMechanicsHelper.GetDefendCollisionResults` normally obtains this policy from `AgentApplyDamageModel.DecideCrushedThrough`. Preserving the original shield-defense fact until the subsequent native body contact allows 35% mitigation without inventing damage or restarting the animation. Native preliminary crush callbacks intentionally skip RegisterBlow; they must not consume the victim's damaging-hit allowance.

Run 46400's unguarded baselines damaged one victim per swing, while both unguarded interventions damaged all three. The shield-left baseline damaged nobody; the shield-left intervention damaged victims 1/2/3 by **35/21/22 HP**, respectively, from native raw blows **99/59/63**, each rounded after multiplying by 0.35. Each victim received exactly one positive-damage hit and one positive unblocked score event during the same native release. No additional-release or contact-progress-reset warning occurred. This establishes the supported route for that controlled geometry.

The shield-right trial remains a failure: only victim 3 lost 1 HP. Its native collision was Blocked at attack progress 0, and **GetDefendCollisionResults never fired** for that right-shield trial; all defenders nevertheless had native DefendShield actions and the requested DefendLeft input. The attacker subsequently entered BlockedMelee. An initial weapon/shield overlap is a hypothesis, not an established engine rule.

Geometry revision 2 moved every target from 1.35 m to **1.65 m forward**, maintaining 0.65 m lateral spacing for all eight baseline/intervention trials. It records exact positions, correct-side shield status, attack/collision bones, body part, attack direction and contact distance along the weapon. [Run 52064](../../evidence/combat-overhaul/runtime/combat/run-52064.jsonl) completed that matrix: left shield cleave again damaged all three targets (30/29/22 HP), but right shield cleave still damaged only victim 3 (5 HP). The right intervention contact now occurred at progress **0.0241674**, `CorrectSideShieldBlock=false`, attack bone 27, collision bone 20 (ArmLeft), and distance along weapon 1.094125 m. The earlier defense-policy callback still did not run. Merely moving the fixture farther away does not solve this path; the failure is not limited to exactly zero progress. A wrong-side/passive shield collision bypassing the managed defense policy is the current supported distinction, not a proven complete native explanation.

Run 52064 subsequently presented a crash modal. Its diagnostic log ends at `combat_complete`; it contains no consumed `requested_clean_exit`. The watchdog log confirms a crash and asks for a dump but provides no exception stack. The engine errors log contains only its initialization header. The general engine log's last FMOD invalid-object-handle message is timestamped 03:11:44, roughly 48 seconds before the 03:12:32 combat completion, so attributing the later crash to that message is unsupported. **Clean shutdown and cause of this late crash remain unverified.** Completion of eight logged trials must not be reported as overall run stability.

Additional read-only investigation of the installed `CustomAgentApplyDamageModel.DecideCrushedThrough` finds a stock requirement for `WeaponFlags.CanCrushThrough`, Swing, AttackUp, non-passive usage, and sufficient energy (58, multiplied by 1.2 against shields). The existing early postfix overrides this model's outcome only when native code calls the managed defense resolver. It cannot affect a path that omits that callback. `CanPenetrateShield` has a verified managed consumer in `Mission.MissileHitCallback`; its name alone does not establish melee penetration. Weapon flags are copied into native `WeaponStatsData` by `MissionWeapon.GetWeaponStatsDataForUsage`, a possible isolated experiment seam, but no changed-flag experiment or reliable wrong-side-shield override is established here. Do not globally mutate shared ItemObject weapon definitions or mark this alternative proven.

## Damage and credit event contract

Do not equate callback count with successful hits. In run 46400 shield-left, victim 2 received a 21-damage OnAgentHit/OnScoreHit pair, then a stock `OnAgentHitBlocked` callback emitted a second **zero-damage**, `isBlocked=true` pair with unchanged HP. Shield-right showed the same zero-event pattern after its 1-damage hit. This is not duplicate HP loss, but a naive ability-XP listener would overcount.

Successful-hit credit must require positive authoritative `damagedHp`, exclude `isBlocked`, and deduplicate by attack instance and victim. Assert one positive damage event and one positive unblocked score event per credited victim; total positive event damage must equal observed HP loss for this nonlethal fixture. Preliminary crush callbacks and zero/blocked notifications grant no successful-hit XP. Do not broadly suppress the stock blocked callback: its managed path also invokes victim AgentComponent.OnHit and CheckToDropFlaggedItem. Whether those effects should be replaced belongs in the production adapter contract. Actual campaign skill XP, ability XP, kills/wounds and integration with other mods remain untested.

## Initial live probe — not acceptance

`evidence/combat-overhaul/runtime/combat/run-44536.jsonl` contains eight trials, eight contact entries and five damage callbacks. In intervention trial 6, ordinary body damage was registered and the postfix returned ContinueChecking with momentum 1; a duplicate victim contact was suppressed without another HP change. However, contacts 2.75 seconds apart reset attack progress within the same trial identifier. A trial ID is not a swing ID. Early action samples remained -1, and input from another game/controller could reach the player-controlled fixture. This run therefore establishes hook execution, not deterministic attack ownership or single-swing correctness.

Every observed contact had `shield=false`. A trial named `shield` is not evidence of a shield collision. No shield-conversion or 35% mitigation claim passes from this run. Baseline later contacts with momentum zero produced zero body damage, supporting the need to test momentum independently from reaction.

That initial revision was subsequently tested as run 42180 above, then replaced by the controlled native AI-input fixture. Actual campaign XP, mounted cases, timed blocking, callback parallelism and full production compatibility remain untested.

## What the installed engine exposes

**N1 — A real typed native-to-managed hit callback exists.** `CoreCallbacksGenerated.Initialize` (`0x06000012`) assigns delegate slot **61** to `Mission_MeleeHitCallback` (`0x06000050`). It resolves Mission/Agent managed IDs, creates a `GameEntity` wrapper only for a nonzero pointer, and forwards `ref AttackCollisionData`, `ref float inOutMomentumRemaining`, `ref MeleeCollisionReaction`, and `ref HitParticleResultData` directly to `Mission.MeleeHitCallback`. The final bool is U1 in the delegate. REA record `ev_bc58f8920cdcc6025c92982a724bb974ffbf5ad908b4b0a2cad708038c5fe04a`. This is a managed patch seam; a native detour is not established as mandatory.

**N2 — Native code consumes the reaction written by managed code.** Existing Ghidra record `ev_a5d0f6a51d2fd78ecc3cf60709740d41f0273e6d709ff684fdb2324fc53f31d3` covers wrapper `0x1805d4e20`; it calls the function pointer at `DAT_180da2970 + 0x210`, bound to slot 61. Retained native binding analysis traces `0x180646770` cases `0x3d/0x43` through the initialized callback table. The collision job at `0x180685a90` invokes this wrapper at `0x180685fa5`. At `0x180686300` it compares the returned local reaction (`[rsp+0x78]`) to **1**, the installed enum's `ContinueChecking`. A non-1 branch reaches `0x18068638e`, clears fields `Agent+0x2d4` and `+0x2d8`, then leaves the loop. Reaction 1 can continue collision-record iteration. REA `ev_928ffaaace9a1b1b4bf00d22acf3a05fecad6ccbba781626439241484daeb138`, byte rechecked above.

That same reaction-1 path searches a short victim-index array at `Agent+0x28` using the count at `+0x68`, replaces a matching entry with the last entry and decrements the count. **Inference:** forcing ContinueChecking may remove native duplicate-hit history. The role and lifetime of that list across all attack paths are not proven here. Implement explicit attack-instance/target deduplication and measure callbacks before trusting native filtering. `SlicedThrough` is enum 0, so it must not be casually treated as synonymous with `ContinueChecking` (1).

**N3 — The native boundary is not the only obstacle.** In `Mission.MeleeHitCallback`, an *incoming* ContinueChecking value skips the main damage path entirely. After normal damage processing, it calls `UpdateMomentumRemaining`, then `MissionGameModels.Current.AgentApplyDamageModel.DecideWeaponCollisionReaction`. Therefore a blanket prefix setting ContinueChecking is an incorrect implementation of cleave: it may prevent damage. Likewise, `Mission.RegisterBlow` excludes `AttackBlockedWithShield`, while `MissionCombatMechanicsHelper.GetAttackCollisionResults` computes shield damage for that flag. Merely changing the outgoing collision reaction does not convert shield contact into mitigated body damage.

**N4 — Momentum is a separate gate.** `AgentApplyDamageModel.CalculateDefaultRemainingMomentum` (`0x06003772`) normally returns zero. Positive continuation is conditional on inflicted damage, no shield/back-shield block, collider agent, no horse charge, and (outside passive attacks) correct weapon bone, non-thrust and `CurrentUsageItem.CanHitMultipleTargets`. It halves residual momentum and zeroes values below 0.25. Native continuation testing must cover both outgoing reaction and remaining momentum; changing only one does not establish multi-target attacks. REA `ev_14aced2f6816fd589f282e04fa49499f7f87a36196d27f8f05b1f103b215448f`.

**N5 — Damage policy already has a chain; preserve vanilla through it.** Sandbox registers `SandboxAgentApplyDamageModel`; NavalDLC registers `NavalAgentApplyDamageModel`. The latter's `DecideWeaponCollisionReaction` (`0x060012f1`) calls `BaseModel.DecideWeaponCollisionReaction`; Sandbox's `0x060007f4` calls `MissionCombatMechanicsHelper.DecideWeaponCollisionReaction`. Naval's `CalculateRemainingMomentum` (`0x060012f3`) delegates then applies additional perk logic. `AgentApplyDamageModel.CalculateDamage` (`0x0600375a`) is nonvirtual and dispatches ignore/amplify/scale/reduce/general-modifier stages through `MissionGameModels.Current.AgentApplyDamageModel`. REA records: Sandbox `ev_0f49d50be02f857151aa37afc1635c597d1dd1ee4ddef4b8866bcd6d60267c13`; Naval `ev_dce1bce53282ae1f5fc406b14205ddeb91612f7e5377a9a202274fbbae7c647d`.

The proposed module must document which stages it replaces for opted-in attacks and delegate excluded attacks to the prior model. Record the **actual runtime model chain**, including other mods, rather than assuming static registration order is the active order. New and vanilla defenders can share a mission; policy belongs to each attack/defender, not an unconditional global replacement.

## Required concrete probes before production attacks

1. **C1 — Native continuation matrix.** A diagnostic weapon hits a line of three agents, first shielded. Record incoming/outgoing collision result, reaction, momentum, attacker/victim IDs, native action index/progress, attack-instance ID, damage and callbacks. Compare unmodified, outgoing SlicedThrough, outgoing ContinueChecking, and explicit residual momentum. Repeat foot/mounted and real weapon swing/custom clip. Pass requires the intended distinct targets receive exactly one hit, no repeated XP, no phantom contact and world geometry still blocks as designed. Do not force the incoming reaction before normal processing.
2. **C2 — Shield-contact adapter.** Trace blocked contact through both `MeleeHitCallback` and `OnAgentHitBlocked`; establish ordering and whether the native engine has already committed shield effects. Design an attack-scoped conversion to mitigated body damage, retaining the original shield-contact fact for defense calculation. Prove one body-damage event, deliberate shield-HP policy, valid hit reaction and continuation. Include back shields, parries/chamber blocks, friendly fire and invulnerability. Do not mutate unrelated actors or treat a read-only collision property as a public setter.
3. **C3 — Exactly-once damage and campaign credit.** A direct `Agent.RegisterBlow` calls `HandleBlow`; it changes health, notifies `Mission.OnAgentHit`, handles death and calls native `HandleBlowAux`. It bypasses the private mission `RegisterBlow` path's other work. Trace `OnRegisterBlow`, `OnAgentHit`, `OnScoreHit`, kills/wounds, skill XP and ability XP for normal/new-mode hits. Build one adapter preserving required side effects once; assigning Health directly is insufficient.
4. **C4 — Native ownership/reaction.** Capture current action/channel/attack state across charge, release, hit, parry, held block, stagger, dismount and death. Prove that custom action playback actually supplies collision evaluation; the managed SetActionChannel wrapper alone cannot prove this. If the native hit producer refuses the custom action, choose either a verified supported action-set route or an owned geometric sweep with the C3 adapter. The alternate route must be explicit, measured and accepted before authoring all animation content.
5. **C5 — Model-chain scope and teardown.** Log current damage models and Harmony patch owners for campaign, custom battle and naval mission. New-mode changes must be actor/attack scoped, non-new actors must match the prior chain, and mission unload/reload must not duplicate registration. Required receipt pins DLL hashes/MVIDs and methods. A changed game build invalidates this static contract until rechecked.

## Ticket decomposition recommended

- Retain the existing broad probe ticket as a **decision/acceptance gate**, with C1–C5 implemented as distinct instrumented prerequisites, not one generic “prove hooks” task.
- Separate continuous-hit reaction/momentum policy from shield contact conversion and from damage/credit integration. The managed/native ordering makes these different failure modes.
- Explicitly own attack-instance lifecycle and deduplication across native callbacks and custom sweep callbacks; include interruption, target despawn and ID reuse.
- Separate “make a clip play” from “make the clip produce timed contacts and legal damage.” Require the latter before animation production batches.
- Native detours remain conditional work, justified only if measured supported seams cannot meet the contract. Preserve a verified decision record either way; “native-only” is not a current user requirement.

## Limits

The prior native review covers a bounded Single0 collision-job path, not every melee worker, projectile, charge or network path. Its retained pseudocode is not original source. Current bytes were checked for the 500-instruction collision-job body, but the helper wrappers' entire bodies were not independently byte-rechecked here; their evidence is bound by the matching whole-file SHA. No claim here establishes runtime thread affinity, all-path deduplication, custom-animation physics, shield writeback timing or performance. Those are named C1–C5 probes, not implementation facts.
