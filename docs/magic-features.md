# Magic features (MMR-289), 2026-10-20

Magic zones carry school-specific features that rise above the ground: obsidian spikes, crystal clusters, monoliths, stone spires, brine springs, wind-carved rocks and bone spires, standing along the veins MMR-288 draws (the owner's choice after acc4: props and features, not reshaped relief, so live mutations get them too). They are built only from installed vanilla meta meshes with runtime multiply tints, plus a vanilla point light and particle systems at each source. Nothing new is published and no TaleWorlds file ships: the policy names meshes and effects only.

Code: `World/MagicFeatures.cs` (pure: the plan along the vein graph, its clearance and budget, the installer), `World/MagicFeaturesRuntime.cs` (engine side, a layer of the magic ground overlay). Policy: `roads.json` `magicFeatures` (strict reader `RoadsPolicy.FeaturesPolicy` in `World/Map2Policies.cs`). Tests: `tools/Test-MagicFeatures.ps1 -BundleA -BundleB [-Evidence evidence/world/magic-features.json]` (`tests/World/MagicFeaturesTests.cs`), policy refusals in `Test-Map2Policies`. ## Feature sets

Per visible school a **source** feature (the largest, with the school's only light and particles), **trunk** features and **debris**. Hidden schools take their look's set (`terrain-materials.json mutation.hiddenLooks`: blood infernal, void umbral, rot water, eldritch weave; default taken: the same meshes and tints). Sizes are map units at the policy's scale (37 px per unit at camera distance 38); tints are ARGB multiply factors from `magic-schools.json`'s colours.

Vanilla has no crystal, obsidian, coral or bone-spire mesh; the crystal stand-in is `icicle`, a curved tapered spike with a near-white texture, so a multiply tint gives any colour.

**Parts.** A feature definition is a list of parts: `meshes` (one chosen per instance), `scale`, `count`, `ring` (the distance from the feature's centre; instances spread round it), `lean` (degrees; outward from the centre for ring parts, along the vein for centre parts; `lean: inward` turns ring parts inward, `lean: wind` leans everything along one hashed wind direction per zone), `tints` (one chosen per instance). Every choice is a hash of the world's source identity and the feature's id. `meshes` gives each mesh's bounding box at scale 1 (metres, read from the `.tpac` mesh records) and `flip` (for a mesh modelled upside down, turned about its side axis; all off). A part stands on the ground: its origin is lifted by its lowest point times its scale (times the cosine of its lean) and sunk `sink` (5%) of its height.

**Lineage** (`evidence/world/magic-features.json` `lineage`, re-read by the harness from the install): each of the 35 meshes is named in the header of an installed `Modules/*/AssetPackages` or `EmAssetPackages` pack, and each of the 8 particle effects is an `<effect name=...>` in the installed `Native/ModuleData`. `psys_blaze_small_1` and `psys_fire_smoke_env_point` are campaign-proven (raided villages, `SettlementVisual`); the others are battle effects whose look at map scale is unverified.

## Placement

`MagicFeatures.Plan(graph, policy, sites)`: pure managed, deterministic, in the canonical physical frame, from MMR-288's `MagicVeinGraph` (`MagicGroundRuntime.Veins` in game).
- **Source**: one per vein source. A source on water or inside a clearance (a live cast's source is the capital, always inside the settlement clearance) moves to the first clear land node of its tree within `sourceSearch` (8) edges, nearest first, then widest. Where the source feature fits nowhere (a crowded or narrow site), the school's trunk features are tried in turn and carry the source's light and particles ("reduced"). A zone wholly on water gets none: its water is MMR-290's.
- **Hidden nodes** (`VeinNode.Hidden`: a generated node under a live cell, where the live zone draws) take no feature of any role (integration of MMR-288's gaps fix).
- **Trunk**: on non-tip land nodes at least `trunkWidth` (3.25) wide, once the arc length along the branch since the last feature reaches the spacing: `trunkSpacing` (4) x (1 + `edgeThinning` x Order / the zone's highest Order) / density, density = (0.5 + 0.5 x potency above the threshold) x (0.5 + 0.5 x the width past `trunkWidth`, relative). So features cluster at the sources and along the wide, potent trunks and thin toward the zone edge. Each is offset across the vein by `lateral` (0.3-0.7) of its half width, the side by hash, and faces along the edge, outward from the source.
- **Debris**: at fork nodes at least `debrisWidth` (3.25) wide that took no trunk feature.
- **Tips** get none, so the veins keep thinning. Default taken: a live cast's tips do take trunk features and its trunk width is `liveTrunkWidth` (2.0, MMR-288's `veins.minWidth`: every live vein), because a cast's veins are few, young and thin.
- **Widths re-checked at integration** (MMR-288's final widths: `minWidth` 2, rims capped at it, wider near the sources). The first values (trunk and debris 2.0, live 1.5) were tuned on the first widths (`minWidth` 1.5): trunk 2.0 then made 23% (A') / 30% (B') of the land nodes eligible. On the final widths 2.0 is the minimum, so 49% / 57% were eligible, rims included, and the reader refused live 1.5 (below `minWidth`). 3.25 restores the tuned shares (the 182nd / 563rd widest eligible node is 3.25 on A' / B'; 30% / 39% of the land nodes), and the counts land near the first tuning's (A' 133, B' 340 features).
- **Gap**: every feature `minGap` (3) units from every other.
- **Order**: generated zones first, then live ones; a cast so never moves a generated feature outside the zones its cells regrow.

**Clear of** (each beyond the feature's footprint radius, its farthest part's ring distance plus that mesh's footprint radius):
- **water**: a water travel cell (sea, lake, river) or a point past a pole line, a water-core sample, ground below the datum (the sea plane shows) or a shown lake plane's box above its ground; checked at the centre and 8 points round the footprint (`water` clearance 0.5 beyond it). Also the opaque core of every water decal (MMR-285's river and fill stamps, `water.opaqueCore` 0.5 x its half extents): the footprint grown by the water clearance must not reach it. A stamp's soft outer box may still lie under a feature (6 parts on A', 22 on B'); MMR-247's river rendering may call for a wider margin;
- **settlements**: every settlement point of the world (`settlement` 5.0). This is the map dressing's rule for ruin markers (`settlement-looks.json ruins.minGap` 5), and it covers the terrain-shaping pads (`terrain-shaping.json pads.radius` town 3.7, castle 2.85, village 2.0) plus the flora clearance's `padMargin` 1 (the trees' rule); `Test-Map2Policies` checks both relations;
- **roads**: every road decal of the map dressing plan (MMR-257: the smoothed route polylines sampled every 1.4 units), as a segment of its half length, beyond its half width plus `road` 0.8;
- **bridges**: every bridge segment (beyond half a piece, 0.6, plus `bridge` 2.0) and each bridge's two bank points;
- **ruins**: every ruin marker of the map dressing plan (MMR-257's hover-labelled ruin prefabs, `MapDressingPlan.Markers`), `ruin` 2.0 beyond the footprint. Vein sources are potency maxima, where FWGC++ ruins often stand; a source on a ruin moves along its tree as it does for a settlement.
Settlements come from MMR-261's mutation context (`MutationSites.Point`, the travel graph's settlement points); roads, bridges, ruin markers, water decals and lake planes from the drawn `MapDressingRuntime.Current.Plan`. Default taken: a world with no mutation context (no settlement table) or no drawn map dressing (its install failed, or the world has no plan) draws no feature rather than risk one on a settlement, road or river (status `none: ...` in the record). MMR-247 (acc5 c30: 11 features stood within 5 units of a hideout, one 0.2 units from it): the hideouts and the retirement retreat the campaign start places (MMR-34, `GeneratedCampaignInitializer`; not travel-graph settlements) are added as `MutationSites.Placed` (WorldReconstruction, from the dataset's start: logical + origin, as they are registered) and kept clear of as settlements. The offline harness cannot build the campaign start, so Test-MagicFeatures checks the rule with stand-in hideouts at every third feature.

**Budget** (`budget`): per world at most `roots` 1,200 feature roots, `entities` 2,400 entities, `lights` 48 point lights and `particles` 48 particle systems; in every `window` 116 x 74 view window (quarter-window steps, columns wrapping) at most 200 roots, 400 entities, 8 lights, 8 particle systems. Every feature counts inside these caps, live ones included. Live features are taken first, then the generated ones; within each, source first, then trunk, then debris, each by priority (potency x width). Past a light or particle cap a feature loses those effects; past a root or entity cap it is thinned. So a cast's light and particles take the slots of the lowest-priority generated sources, and any number of stacked casts stays within the caps (the harness checks the Nerdun cast and eight stacked copies of it). Placement still plans live zones after generated ones, so a cast never moves a generated feature; only the caps may take a generated source's effects or, in a full window, a generated feature.

## Engine side and lifetime

`MagicFeaturesRuntime.Layer` is registered in `MagicGroundRuntime.Layers` (MMR-288's `IMagicVeinLayer` hook; the one initializer holds it first, then MMR-290's magic water), so it follows the overlay on the engine thread:
- **Map load** (`GeneratedMapScene.AfterLoad`, after the map dressing and the magic ground decals): plans the loaded map's full vein graph (generated zones and the live state MMR-261 restored) and draws what the campaign time shows.
- **Live mutation** (MMR-261's safe point: `MagicGroundRuntime.Set` -> the layer's `Changed`): a new graph is planned again and the installer adds and removes only the features whose key changed (the cast's and its regrown zones'). A cast's features appear with its veins: a feature shows when its anchor node is revealed (MMR-288's reveal over `revealHours`, 48 h). The overlay calls its layers at its reveal steps, `MagicGroundPlan.NextReveal`, which covers the vein graph's nodes as well as the land stamps (the same change as MMR-290's), so a feature whose node owns no land stamp, or lies past the cast's last land stamp, still shows at its own time.
- **Continue**: the stored mutation document (`_mnblWorldMutations`) restores the live cells and nodes before the map loads (`MagicGroundRuntime.Restore`); the install plans the same graph, so the same features come back, fully grown once the reveal time has passed.
- **Seams**: each entity is drawn at its periodic image nearest the camera and moved on a camera wrap (`RefreshImages`); no copy per image.
- **Release**: `Release(true)` (a new Begin on the same live scene) removes every entity with `Scene.RemoveEntity`; `Release(false)` (`GeneratedMapScene.Destroy`, after `MapScreen.OnFinalize`'s `Scene.ClearAll` took them, MMR-262) only lets go of the handles, with no engine call. The second after the first is a no-op.
- **Entities**: every entity is a separate scene root, never inside a baked settlement: `GameEntity.CreateEmpty`, `AddMultiMesh(MetaMesh.GetCopy(mesh, false, true))`, `SetFactorColor(tint)` (skipped for white), `SetGlobalFrame` (the part's axes, scale included, at the ground plus its lift), `SetReadyToRender(true)`. A source's effects are one more root with `Light.CreatePointLight` (colour, intensity, `SetLightFlicker(0.6, 1.0)` as `map_icon_fx_village_plunder`) and `AddParticleSystemComponent` (SandBox.View's campaign-map call). No prefab: every candidate prefab carries a physics body. A mesh that does not resolve draws nothing and is counted; the rest of its feature stands.
- **Record**: `MagicFeaturesRuntime.Record` (status, settlement/road/bridge counts, the plan's counts, the installer's created/removed/dropped/missing meshes, install time).
