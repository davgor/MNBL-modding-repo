## Design

Represent distant troops as formation populations, positions and combat state. Spend native agent slots on the area around the player. Promotion reserves a soldier, spawns a real agent and commits the reservation. Failed spawns return the reservation. Demotion waits for the engine's removal callback and next-tick reconciliation before returning the soldier to the aggregate population. An inactive, retiring agent still occupies a native slot until deletion: the adapter retains its binding and separately gates all spawns against the complete engine entity list. Dead soldiers are casualties, not replacement copies. A rider and horse cost two slots in the core.

This changes the scaling variable from total soldiers to formation count plus active soldiers. It does **not** make infinite resources possible. A million logical troops, a million visible figures and a million individually simulated fighters are three different targets.

The reusable implementation is `src/MNBL.BattleScale/Core/BattleSimulation.cs`. It has no game or Harmony dependency. A formation's initial population must always equal ready + reserved + active + killed + wounded. Tickets are stable logical identities, never reused engine agent indices. Planning is transactional: every offered ticket must be committed or cancelled. A recommended demotion does not release its active budget until the engine confirms removal from combat. Native occupancy is a separate, stricter check. All operations are mission-thread-owned.

The demonstration has four stationary 25,000-troop formations, two opposing pairs at installed benchmark scene landmarks. It uses at most 128 active infantry agents plus the player, with a separate ceiling of 257 total native entities including retiring agents and bodies. Actual engine count independently gates every spawn. Spawn bursts are limited to eight per planning pass. An automated probe visits both areas, waits for confirmed materialization, returns to the first and then observes native combat for 30 seconds. Interactive play starts combat immediately.

## Native pool expansion: offline patch preparation

**Restricted infantry runtime acceptance passed; general safety remains unverified.** The historical r10 1,676-edit candidate expands the five Mission pools and repairs a separate renderer bone-mapping limit. Those historical failed receipts remain unchanged. Historical infantry evidence is `native-pool-runtime-r10.json` and `native-pool-run-r10/`; `native-pool-runtime-diagnosis.json` retains the r7 fault.

The first expansion target is **4,080 native slots**. Doubling 2,040 preserves the existing cleanup loops that process three and ten slots per iteration. The five inline pools retain their element strides: two at `0xc60`, one at `0x230`, and two at `0x2c8`. Each also needs a larger signed-short index table, relocated count/high-water fields, updated allocation guards and sentinels, and matching constructors and destructors. Correction to earlier notes: the second pool begins at Mission+`0x62da50`, after eight bytes of padding, not `0x62da48`.

Expansion changes the addresses of later Mission members. Every affected access needs its owning pointer established first: Mission-relative, pool-relative and Agent-relative offsets have different meanings. Image lookup tables, stack locals, unrelated large Scene resources and misdecoded switch data produced many false matches in the initial inventory. A global search-and-replace of 2,040 or of large offsets would corrupt unrelated code.

Additional required storage and lifecycle changes found so far:

- Three job containers have 127 page pointers and 127 flags, with 16 records per page. Expand each to 255 pages, preserving its record stride and relocating its flags. This adds `0xd80` bytes beyond the five-pool expansion. Their three constructor loops and shared destructor require explicit instruction rewrites: `CMP EDX/EBX,127` becomes `CMP DL/BL,255`. Simply changing the immediate byte to `ff` would compare against signed -1.
- Two frame-local heap allocations hold 2,040 Agent pointers each. Their allocation sizes must change from `0x3fc0` to `0x7f80`. The reviewed producers append at most once per input iteration; the dispatcher partitions iterations atomically and waits for completion. The proposed test uses `/maxThreadCount 16` and verifies the native task-pool storage, because the collision-queue reservation proof has a concurrency bound.
- Exception-cleanup metadata identifies 70 saved-Mission field adjustments, including 66 after the first growth boundary, and three full-array cleanup paths. Their field addresses and destruction counts must agree with normal cleanup. Keep instruction lengths and unwind metadata consistent.
- The three adjacent inline pointer vectors already spill to heap storage. Preserve their 2,040-entry inline limits and relative metadata offsets. Two later 255-page collision queues use a different 64-record page geometry; preserve those limits and audit their admission guards separately.

The resulting layout calculation is **35,366,000 bytes per Mission**, up from 18,369,344, before separately allocated Agent resources and temporary buffers. Build the final storage before any Agent pointers escape; never relocate a live Mission. The calculation proves storage geometry, not completeness of the native patch.

Managed contracts also constrain this test profile. Agent objects retain direct pointers to native fields. Spawn-limit caches must observe the patched getter before initialization. The normal troop quota reserves half the native capacity for mounts, so a 4,080-slot engine does not automatically produce 4,080 soldiers through the standard spawner. Multiplayer and replay index serialization has a narrower encoding. The manifest now includes a six-byte guard at `SetGameType` that returns for Single mode zero and terminates immediately for any nonzero value. Static review verifies the original zero initialization and identified writers. The probe additionally rejects recording before opening its Mission. The permitted Single0 path passed in r10; nonzero-mode termination and capsule-overflow failure paths remain untested in game.

## What remains before this can power real mods

1. Persistent identities and complete state across tiers: health, ammunition, shield/weapon state, horses, morale, formations, orders and retreat. The current adapter conservatively refuses demotion of injured or previously engaged agents; it does not solve state preservation.
2. A moving formation simulation with spatial indexing, terrain, line of sight, missile exchange and routing. The current far-combat model is stationary, explicit-target, frontage-limited attrition. Its casualty rates are uncalibrated. It excludes near or partly materialized formations to prevent double damage; consequently near reserves do not fight abstractly.
3. Seamless visibility and allocation. The laboratory spawns agents visibly and has no impostors or distant crowd rendering. Protected/engaged units can occupy the whole pool. Budget saturation currently waits rather than violating the limit.
4. Campaign transaction boundaries. Campaign troop rosters, heroes, casualties, wounded, prisoners, XP, loot and retreat outcomes must have one authoritative reconciliation path. The laboratory does not update campaign data or saves and does not support campaign battles, sieges or multiplayer.
5. Broader performance and stability work beyond the completed five-minute trials: GPU timing, matched baselines, different assets/maps, native AI/physics/animation costs, player travel, ranged attacks across simulation tiers and prolonged capacity reuse.
6. A general extension framework: versioned capability contracts, patch conflict handling, stable managed/native interop and subsystem replacement. Native detours need a configured analyzer and verified ABI/lifetime evidence. This experiment does not supply unverified native hooks.

The intended next architecture is formation-level decisions and shared navigation for distant troops, ordinary individual combat near the player, and a renderer for distant armies. Harmony can remain the managed patch mechanism while a separate, version-specific native layer adds capabilities that cannot be reached through managed APIs.
